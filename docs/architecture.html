Indic Call Translator

Two people on a browser call each speak their own Indian language and hear the other translated into the language they chose, with live captions. Everything runs on one GPU host: three model services and a LiveKit-based call stack in front of them.

Decisions that shape it

  • Private tracks, not mixingThe worker publishes one translated track per listener and LiveKit's subscription permissions keep it theirs. Same-language pairs skip translation and hear each other's own voice.
  • The model sets the routesarvam-translate translates English to and from the Indian languages. Between two Indian languages the worker goes through English, as the model card says.
  • NVIDIA NIM wherever NVIDIA supports itAI4Bharat's IndicConformer models, converted with NVIDIA's nemo2riva and riva-build, run in the Riva ASR NIM; sarvam-translate and indic-speak's speech LM run on the model-free LLM NIM. Only the VAD that cuts calls into utterances and indic-speak's audio decoder are our own code.
  • Self-healing callsIf the worker restarts or crashes, the web app's watchdog sends a new translator into any room that lost one, within about 10 to 15 seconds.
  • No feedback loopsThe worker cancels everything it plays out of every microphone and drops utterances whose loudness tracks the playout, so translated speech from a nearby speaker is never translated again.
  • Models stay privateThe model services listen on localhost only. The network sees the HTTPS proxy on 8443, LiveKit's TCP 7881 and UDP 50000 to 50200.
  • Speech synthesis sets capacityAbout 6 to 7 simultaneous real-time voices per GPU. Speech recognition handles about 44 utterances a second.
  • Streaming everywhere it helpsCaptions appear while people talk (the utterance so far is re-transcribed every half second), translations stream word by word, and speech starts about 0.4 s after the text is ready.
